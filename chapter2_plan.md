## Chapter 2: Lessons 20–26

Chapter 2 of the book (*Čia mano draugas*, pp. 27–41) teaches five things: asking how someone is, calling people by name, introducing a friend, saying where you're from, and saying which languages you speak and understand. At 4–6 new items a lesson that is seven lessons, 20–26, adding 37 items. Lesson 26 is lighter on new words and ends with the chapter's reading text as a cumulative conversation.

**What carries over from chapter 1 and is reused in every lesson:** *aš esu / tu esi / jis, ji yra / mes esame / jūs esate* and the negatives, *kalbu / kalbate / nekalbu*, *žinau / nežinau*, *studentas / studentė*, *dėstytojas / dėstytoja*, *lietuvis / lietuvė*, *australas / australė*, *mano / tavo / jūsų*, *koks / kokia*, *kas / kur*, *ar … ar*, *o*, *taip pat*, *atsiprašau*, *ačiū / prašom*, *labas / iki*, *pas mane / pas jus / pas tave*.

**Two patterns the chapter rests on, each given its own lesson:** the way a name changes when you call someone (Tomas → *Tomai!*, Lesson 21), and the way a place changes after *iš*, "from" (Lietuva → *iš Lietuvos*, Lessons 23–24). Both are first cases of noun endings changing, so each is taught through a handful of names and places you'll use, not as a table.

## Lesson 20: "Kaip sekasi?" (how are you)

**Goal:** ask how someone is, answer on a scale from great to bad, and ask back. **Builds on:** *Labas!* / *Laba diena*, *ačiū*, *o jūs? / o tu?*, *kalbu / kalbate*, *truputį*, *taip pat*.

**Pronunciation focus:** *ai* in *kaip* and *gerai* (like *eye*, but shorter); *ui* in *puikiai*; *šiaip sau* is two syllables, "shyp sow"; soft *k* in *puikiai* (PUI-kiai, two syllables).

**Warm-up (Lesson 18):** I'm Australian → a woman says "I'm Lithuanian" → we are here → what's your surname? → sorry! → no problem.

**Opening conversation**

- Andrius: Labas, Rūta! Kaip sekasi?
- Rūta: Ačiū, gerai! O kaip tau?
- Andrius: Ačiū, taip pat gerai!

**New items (6)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kaip sekasi? | KAIP SE-ka-si? | How are you? / How are things? | si → ka-si → SE-ka-si |
| Ačiū, gerai. | A-čiū, ge-RAI | Fine, thanks. | RAI → ge-RAI |
| puikiai / labai gerai | PUI-kiai / la-BAI ge-RAI | great / very well | kiai → PUI-kiai |
| šiaip sau | ŠIAIP SAU | so-so | – |
| blogai | blo-GAI | bad(ly) | GAI → blo-GAI |
| O kaip jums? / O kaip tau? | o KAIP JUMS? / o KAIP TAU? | And you? (polite / friendly) | *jums* and *tau* are the "to you" forms; *tau* pairs with *pas tave* |

**One-line grammar:** *Kaip sekasi?* literally asks "how is it going?", so it needs no "you"; to ask back you say *O kaip jums?* (polite) or *O kaip tau?* (friendly).

**New ways to use what you know**

- *gerai* with a verb you know: *Jūs gerai kalbate lietuviškai.* (You speak Lithuanian well.) → *Ne, truputį.*
- *taip pat* in the reply: *Ačiū, taip pat gerai.*
- *o* to contrast: *Aš gerai, o Jonas šiaip sau.*
- *labai* on its own was in *labai malonu*; now *labai gerai*.
- Full polite version from the book: *Kaip jums sekasi?*

**Mock conversations**

1. **Two friends** — *Labas! Kaip sekasi? — Ačiū, puikiai! O kaip tau? — Šiaip sau.* (Names in the vocative, *Tomai!*, wait for Lesson 21.)
2. **Student and teacher** — *Laba diena. Kaip jums sekasi? — Ačiū, gerai. O kaip jums? — Ačiū, taip pat gerai.*
3. **Compliment** — *Jūs gerai kalbate lietuviškai! — Ačiū. Ne, truputį. O jūs gerai kalbate!*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** ask a friend how she is → say "fine, thanks, and you?" (friendly) → say "so-so" → say "very well" → tell your teacher she speaks Lithuanian well → say "I'm well, and Jonas is so-so."

**Book follow-up:** chapter 2, *Frazės* (p. 28), left column, first block only. Dialogue 1 (p. 29). Exercise 14, item 1 (p. 40). (Dialogue 2 uses *ponia / pone* and names in the vocative: next lesson.)

## Lesson 21: "Labas, Tomai!" (calling people by name; ponas, ponia)

**Goal:** greet and address people by name the way Lithuanians do, and use *Mr* and *Mrs*. **Builds on:** every greeting, *Kaip sekasi?*, *atsiprašau*, *ačiū*, the names you've met (Tomas, Jonas, Andrius, Paulius, Eglė, Rasa, Lina, Ieva, Inga).

**Pronunciation focus:** *-ai* at the end of *Tomai, Jonai* (stress stays where it was in the name); *-au* in *Pauliau, Andriau*; *pone* has two syllables, PO-ne.

**Warm-up (Lesson 20):** how are you? → fine, thanks → and you? (polite, then friendly) → so-so → you speak Lithuanian well.

**Opening conversation**

- Tomas: Kaip jums sekasi, ponia Agne?
- Agnė: Ačiū, pone Tomai, gerai.

**New items (5)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tomai! Jonai! | TO-mai, JO-nai | Tomas! Jonas! (calling) | names in *-as* → *-ai* |
| Pauliau! Andriau! | PAU-liau, AN-driau | Paulius! Andrius! | names in *-ius* → *-iau* |
| Egle! / Rasa! Lina! | EG-le / RA-sa | Eglė! / Rasa! | *-ė* → *-e*; *-a* names don't change |
| ponas → pone | PO-nas → PO-ne | Mr (calling: *pone Tomai*) | – |
| ponia | po-NIA | Mrs / Ms (*ponia Agne*) | NIA → po-NIA |

**One-line grammar:** when you speak *to* someone, their name takes the "calling" ending; when you speak *about* them, it stays as it is: *Labas, Tomai!* but *Tomas yra čia.*

**New ways to use what you know**

- Every known phrase now addressed by name: *Atsiprašau, pone Jonai, kur yra Rasa?* / *Ačiū, Egle.* / *Iki, Pauliau!*
- Polite address to a teacher: *Laba diena, ponia Ieva.* (*Ieva* ends in *-a*, so it doesn't change.)
- Contrast *speaking to* and *speaking about*: *Labas, Andriau! — Kur yra Andrius? — Andrius yra ten.*
- The learner's own name: practise calling yourself as a Lithuanian would (narrator explains the rule for the learner's name ending).

**Mock conversations**

1. **In the corridor** — *Labas, Tomai! Kaip sekasi? — Ačiū, Egle, gerai. O kaip tau? — Puikiai!*
2. **Formal** — *Laba diena, pone Jonai. — Laba diena, ponia Rasa. Kaip jums sekasi? — Ačiū, gerai.*
3. **Who's calling whom** — *Pauliau! Kur tu esi? — Aš čia, Lina! — Ar Andrius yra pas tave? — Ne, jis nėra čia.*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** call out to Jonas → greet Eglė by name and ask how she is → say "thank you, Mr Tomas" → politely ask Mrs Rasa where the teacher is → say bye to Paulius → say "Andrius is here" (about him), then call him.

**Book follow-up:** dialogue 2 (p. 29), grammar 1 (p. 32), exercise 3 (p. 34, the names you know: Jonas, Eglė, Inga, Paulius, Lukas, Viktorija) and exercise 4 (p. 35).

## Lesson 22: "Čia mano draugas" (introducing a friend; tu kalbi)

**Goal:** introduce a friend, respond to an introduction briefly, and ask a friend whether they speak Lithuanian using the friendly verb form. **Builds on:** *čia*, *mano / tavo / jūsų*, *jis / ji yra*, *studentas / studentė*, *lietuvis / lietuvė*, *labai malonu*, names in the vocative.

**Pronunciation focus:** *au* in *draugas* (one sound, "ow"); *draugė* ends in flat *ė*; *kalbi* has final stress, kal-BI, like *esi*.

**Warm-up (Lesson 21):** call Tomas → call Eglė → say "Mr Jonas" → "Mrs Rasa" → greet Paulius and ask how he is.

**Opening conversation**

- Povilas: Marija, čia mano draugas Petras. Jis yra studentas.
- Marija: Malonu!
- Petras: Man taip pat malonu!

**New items (5)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| draugas | DRAU-gas | friend (male) | gas → DRAU-gas |
| draugė | drau-GĖ | friend (female) | GĖ → drau-GĖ |
| Čia mano draugas … | ČIA MA-no DRAU-gas | This is my friend … | *čia* in a new job: "here is / this is" |
| Malonu. | ma-lo-NU | Nice (to meet you). | the short form of *labai malonu* |
| tu kalbi / nekalbi | tu kal-BI | you speak / don't speak (friendly) | BI → kal-BI; compare *jūs kalbate* |

**One-line grammar:** *čia* means "here", and at the start of a sentence it also means "this is": *Čia mano draugė Lina.*

**New ways to use what you know**

- *čia* as "this is" with anything known: *Čia mano dėstytoja.* / *Čia Jonas.*
- Introduce with a fact: *Čia mano draugas Petras. Jis yra lietuvis.* / *Čia mano draugė Ana. Ji yra australė.*
- Ask about a friend: *Ar jis tavo draugas?* / *Ar ji jūsų draugė?* → *Taip, ji mano draugė.*
- Friendly questions complete: *Ar tu kalbi lietuviškai? — Taip, truputį. O tu?*
- *o* contrast: *Jis mano draugas, o ji mano dėstytoja.*

**Mock conversations**

1. **At a party** — Povilas introduces Petras to you; you reply *Malonu!* and ask *Ar jūs kalbate lietuviškai?*; Petras: *Taip, aš lietuvis.*
2. **You introduce** — *Lina, čia mano draugė Eglė. Ji yra studentė. — Malonu! Ar tu kalbi lietuviškai? — Taip, truputį.*
3. **Who is that?** — *Kas jis? — Čia mano draugas Tomas. — Ar jis lietuvis? — Ne, australas.*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** introduce your friend Petras → introduce your friend Eglė and say she's a student → say "nice" → ask a friend if she speaks Lithuanian → ask a friend "is he your friend?" → say "this is my teacher, Mrs Ieva."

**Book follow-up:** dialogue 6 (p. 29) and the *Čia mano draugas / draugė* lines on p. 28. In the vocabulary (p. 31), *draugas, draugė*.

## Lesson 23: "Iš kur jūs esate?" (where you're from: countries)

**Goal:** ask where someone is from and answer with a country. **Builds on:** *kur*, *jūs esate / tu esi*, *aš esu*, *jis / ji yra*, *lietuvis / australas*, *draugas*.

**Pronunciation focus:** *iš* is "ish", very short; *Lietuvos* has final stress, lie-tu-VOS; *Australijos* is four syllables, aus-TRA-li-jos.

**Warm-up (Lesson 22):** this is my friend Petras → this is my friend Eglė → nice → do you speak Lithuanian? (friendly) → is he your friend?

**Opening conversation**

- Valerija: Aš esu iš Ukrainos. Iš kur tu esi, Tomai?
- Tomas: Aš iš Australijos.

**New items (5)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iš kur jūs esate? / Iš kur tu esi? | iš KUR JŪS E-sa-te? | Where are you from? | *iš* + *kur* |
| iš Lietuvos | iš lie-tu-VOS | from Lithuania | VOS → tu-VOS → lie-tu-VOS |
| iš Australijos | iš aus-TRA-li-jos | from Australia | jos → li-jos → TRA-li-jos → aus-TRA-li-jos |
| iš Vokietijos | iš vo-kie-TI-jos | from Germany | jos → TI-jos → kie-TI-jos → vo-kie-TI-jos |
| iš Anglijos | iš AN-gli-jos | from England | jos → gli-jos → AN-gli-jos |

**One-line grammar:** after *iš*, a country ending in *-a* changes to *-os*: Lietuva → *iš Lietuvos*, Australija → *iš Australijos*. (Lithuanian nouns change their ending to show their role; this is the first such change you've met for places.)

**New ways to use what you know**

- Country and nationality together: *Aš esu australas. Aš esu iš Australijos.*
- About others: *Jis yra iš Lietuvos.* / *Ji yra iš Vokietijos.* / *Mano draugė yra iš Anglijos.*
- Short form, no verb: *Aš iš Australijos. O tu?*
- Negatives: *Aš nesu iš Lietuvos.* / *Jis nėra iš Anglijos.*
- *ar … ar*: *Ar jūs esate iš Anglijos ar iš Australijos?*
- *Iš kur* with *žinau*: *Aš nežinau, iš kur jis.*

**Mock conversations**

1. **New acquaintance** — *Iš kur jūs esate? — Aš esu iš Australijos, o jūs? — Aš iš Lietuvos.*
2. **About a friend** — *Čia mano draugas Karstenas. — Malonu! Iš kur jis? — Jis yra iš Vokietijos.*
3. **Guessing wrong** — *Ar tu iš Anglijos? — Ne, aš nesu iš Anglijos. Aš iš Australijos. — Atsiprašau! — Nieko tokio.*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** ask a stranger where he's from → say you're from Australia → ask a friend where she's from → say your friend is from Germany → ask "are you from England or from Australia?" → say "I don't know where she's from."

**Book follow-up:** dialogues 3 and 4 (p. 29); grammar 2 (p. 32), *-a → -os* row only; exercise 5 (p. 35), the *šalis* column, countries in *-a* and *-ija* only.

## Lesson 24: "Iš Perto, iš Vilniaus" (cities; anglas, vokietis)

**Goal:** say which city you're from, and add two nationality pairs so you can describe the people you meet. **Builds on:** *iš* + country, *lietuvis / lietuvė*, *australas / australė*, *studentas / studentė* pattern.

**Pronunciation focus:** *Vilniaus* is two syllables, VIL-niaus (soft *n*); *Kauno* with *au* as "ow"; *vokietis* has the *ie* glide, vo-KIE-tis.

**Warm-up (Lesson 23):** where are you from? → I'm from Australia → he's from Lithuania → she's from Germany → are you from England or Australia?

**Opening conversation**

- Administratorė: Iš kur jūs esate, pone Karstenai?
- Karstenas: Aš esu iš Vokietijos.
- Administratorė: Iš Berlyno?
- Karstenas: Ne, iš Frankfurto.

**New items (5)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| iš Perto, iš Kauno | iš PER-to, iš KAU-no | from Perth, from Kaunas | cities in *-as* → *-o* |
| iš Vilniaus | iš VIL-niaus | from Vilnius | *-ius* → *-iaus* (like Paulius → Pauliau, a different ending but the same kind of change) |
| iš Berlyno, iš Londono | iš ber-LY-no, iš LON-do-no | from Berlin, from London | *-as* → *-o* again |
| anglas / anglė | AN-glas / AN-glė | English man / woman | glas → AN-glas |
| vokietis / vokietė | vo-KIE-tis / vo-KIE-tė | German man / woman | tis → KIE-tis → vo-KIE-tis |

**One-line grammar:** cities in *-as* take *-o* after *iš* (Pertas → *iš Perto*); *Vilnius* takes *-iaus*. Nationality words follow the pattern you know: *-as / -ė*, or *-is / -ė* (vokietis / vokietė, like lietuvis / lietuvė).

**New ways to use what you know**

- Country, then city: *Aš esu iš Australijos, iš Perto.*
- Nationality + city: *Jis yra vokietis. Jis yra iš Berlyno.* / *Ji yra anglė, iš Londono.*
- Questions: *Iš Vilniaus? — Ne, iš Kauno.* / *Ar tu iš Perto?*
- Introductions with everything so far: *Čia mano draugė Ana. Ji yra anglė, iš Londono.*
- *nežinau*: *Aš nežinau, iš kur ji. Iš Anglijos ar iš Australijos?*

**Mock conversations**

1. **The follow-up question** — *Iš kur jūs esate? — Iš Australijos. — Iš Perto? — Taip, iš Perto.*
2. **Describing a friend** — *Kas ji? — Čia mano draugė Ana. Ji yra anglė. — Iš Londono? — Ne, ji nėra iš Londono.*
3. **Front desk** (book dialogue 3) — *Iš kur jūs esate, pone Karstenai? — Aš esu iš Vokietijos. — Iš Berlyno? — Ne.*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** say you're from Australia, from Perth → ask "from Vilnius?" → say "no, from Kaunas" → say she's English, from London → say he's German → introduce your friend Karstenas, a German from Berlin.

**Book follow-up:** dialogues 3 and 5 (p. 29); grammar 2 (p. 32), the *-as → -o* and *-us → -aus* rows; exercise 5 (p. 35), the *sostinė* column, cities in *-as* only; exercise 6 (p. 36), items 1 and 5.

## Lesson 25: "Kaip jūs kalbate?" (languages; he speaks, we speak; but)

**Goal:** say which languages you, your friends and your group speak, and ask others. **Builds on:** *kalbu / kalbi / kalbate*, *lietuviškai*, *anglas, vokietis, lietuvis*, *truputį*, *gerai*, *taip pat*.

**Pronunciation focus:** *-iškai* always carries the stress before it on the root: AN-gliš-kai, VO-kiš-kai, RU-siš-kai; *bet* is short, like English *bet*.

**Warm-up (Lesson 24):** I'm from Australia, from Perth → from Vilnius? → she's English → he's German, from Berlin → do you speak Lithuanian? (friendly).

**Opening conversation**

- Ignas: Aš kalbu lietuviškai, rusiškai ir angliškai. O jūs, Viktorija?
- Viktorija: Aš kalbu angliškai ir lietuviškai. Rusiškai truputį, bet nekalbu.

**New items (6)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| angliškai | AN-gliš-kai | (in) English | kai → gliš-kai → AN-gliš-kai; from *anglas* |
| vokiškai, rusiškai | VO-kiš-kai, RU-siš-kai | (in) German, (in) Russian | from *vokietis*, *rusas* |
| jis / ji kalba | JIS KAL-ba | he / she speaks | the *jis / ji* form has no ending: *kalba* |
| mes kalbame | MES KAL-ba-me | we speak | me → ba-me → KAL-ba-me |
| Kaip jūs kalbate? | KAIP JŪS KAL-ba-te? | What languages do you speak? | *kaip* in a new job |
| bet | BET | but | – |

**One-line grammar:** the full set for "speak": *kalbu, kalbi, kalba, kalbame, kalbate*. Every regular verb you meet from now on follows the same endings *-u, -i, -a, -ame, -ate*.

**New ways to use what you know**

- Nationality → language: *Jis yra anglas. Jis kalba angliškai.*
- *bet* joining two known facts: *Aš esu australas, bet kalbu truputį lietuviškai.* / *Ji yra lietuvė, bet gerai kalba angliškai.*
- Negatives: *Mes nekalbame vokiškai.* / *Jis nekalba lietuviškai.*
- *kaip* twice: *Kaip sekasi?* vs *Kaip jūs kalbate?*
- Lists with *ir*: *Aš kalbu angliškai ir truputį lietuviškai.*
- About the group: *Mes kalbame angliškai. O jūs?*

**Mock conversations**

1. **Comparing languages** (book dialogue 8) — *Aš kalbu lietuviškai, rusiškai ir angliškai. O jūs? — Aš kalbu angliškai ir truputį lietuviškai.*
2. **About a friend** — *Ar tavo draugas kalba lietuviškai? — Ne, jis nekalba lietuviškai. Jis kalba angliškai.*
3. **The group** — *Kaip jūs kalbate? — Mes kalbame angliškai, bet truputį lietuviškai taip pat.*

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** say you speak English and a little Lithuanian → say he's German and speaks German → say "we speak English" → ask a group what languages they speak → say "I'm Australian, but I speak a little Lithuanian" → say "my friend doesn't speak Lithuanian."

**Book follow-up:** dialogues 7, 8, 9 (p. 30); grammar 3 and 4 (p. 33); exercise 7 (p. 37, the rows you know: anglas, lietuvis, vokietis, rusas); exercise 10 (p. 38), the *kalbėti* half; exercise 11 (p. 38).

## Lesson 26: "Ar aišku?" (understanding; classroom phrases; chapter 2 review)

**Goal:** say what you understand, ask for a repeat or a slower pace, and hold a full conversation with everything from chapters 1 and 2. **Builds on:** the *kalbėti* endings (now applied to a second verb), *prašom*, *atsiprašau*, *gerai*, *labai*, *bet*.

**Pronunciation focus:** *suprantu* has final stress, su-pran-TU; *aišku* starts with *ai*; *lėčiau* is two syllables, LĖ-čiau; *pakartoti* has stress on *to*, pa-kar-TO-ti.

**Warm-up (Lesson 25):** I speak English → he speaks German → we speak English → what languages do you speak? → I'm Australian, but I speak a little Lithuanian.

**Opening conversation**

- Dėstytoja: Ar jūs suprantate lietuviškai?
- Studentas: Taip, truputį suprantu. Prašom kalbėti lėčiau.
- Dėstytoja: Gerai. Ar aišku?
- Studentas: Taip, dabar aišku. Ačiū.

**New items (5)**

| Lithuanian | Say it | English | Build-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| suprantu, supranti, supranta, suprantame, suprantate | su-pran-TU … su-PRAN-ta-te | I / you / he, she / we / you understand | TU → pran-TU → su-pran-TU; same endings as *kalbu* |
| Ar aišku? Taip, aišku. / Ne, neaišku. | ar AIŠ-ku? | Is that clear? Yes, clear. / No, not clear. | ku → AIŠ-ku |
| Prašom pakartoti. | PRA-šom pa-kar-TO-ti | Please repeat. | ti → TO-ti → kar-TO-ti → pa-kar-TO-ti |
| Prašom kalbėti lėčiau. | PRA-šom kal-BĖ-ti LĖ-čiau | Please speak more slowly. | čiau → LĖ-čiau; *kalbėti* = "to speak" |
| dabar | da-BAR | now | BAR → da-BAR |

**One-line grammar:** *kalbėti* is the dictionary form, "to speak"; after *prašom* ("please") you use that form. *Suprasti*, "to understand", takes the same personal endings you learned for *kalbėti*.

**New ways to use what you know**

- Understand vs speak: *Aš suprantu lietuviškai, bet nekalbu.* / *Truputį suprantu rusiškai.*
- The learner's survival kit, all in one: *Atsiprašau, aš nesuprantu. Prašom pakartoti. Prašom kalbėti lėčiau. … Ačiū, dabar aišku.*
- About others: *Mano draugas supranta lietuviškai, bet nekalba.*
- *gerai* as "OK": *Prašom kalbėti lėčiau. — Gerai.*
- *dabar* with known words: *Dabar aš žinau.* / *Dabar aišku.*

**Mock conversations**

1. **In class** — *Ar aišku? — Ne, neaišku. Prašom pakartoti. — [repeat] — Taip, dabar aišku. Ačiū.*
2. **Too fast** — *Atsiprašau, aš nesuprantu. Prašom kalbėti lėčiau. — Gerai. Iš kur jūs esate? — Dabar suprantu! Iš Australijos.*
3. **Cumulative conversation (last 8 minutes), from the book's reading text (p. 41):** Lina asks Tomas whether Maiklas is his friend, whether he speaks Lithuanian, how they speak together, and who understands Spanish; the lesson version uses only known languages (Lithuanian, English, German). You play Tomas, then Lina; the lesson ends with an open conversation that uses everything from both chapters.

**Build-your-own-sentence prompts:** say "I don't understand" → ask for a repeat → ask her to speak more slowly → say "now it's clear" → say "my friend understands Lithuanian but doesn't speak it" → say "we understand a little German."

**Book follow-up:** the *Ar aišku?* block on p. 28; exercise 10 (p. 38), the *suprasti* half; exercises 12, 13 (p. 39) and 15 (p. 40); then the reading text (p. 41), marking who speaks what.

## Word register: Lessons 20–26

Lessons 20–26 add 37 items, 5–6 per lesson, on top of Lessons 11–18. A word is used in a lesson only if it appears in that lesson's row or an earlier one. Names in the vocative (Tomai, Egle…) are introduced as a pattern in Lesson 21 and used freely from then on.

| Check | Result |

The vocabulary for 19–25 is now in the transcript checker, so the audio scripts will be verified mechanically as the chapter 1 scripts were. |
| Known words in new jobs | *čia* → "this is" (L21); *kaip* → "which languages" (L24); *gerai* → "well" with verbs (L19) and "OK" (L25); *labai* → *labai gerai*; *prašom* → "please" + infinitive (L25); *o* for turning questions back in every lesson. |
| Gender agreement | Every new noun is introduced as a pair (*draugas / draugė*, *anglas / anglė*, *vokietis / vokietė*), and vocatives are grouped by ending so male and female names are both drilled. Audio: male voice for the learner, female voice for women's lines, as in chapter 1. |

| Pronunciation focus per lesson | Each lesson names 2–3 sounds; new this chapter: *ai / au / ui* diphthongs, soft consonants before *i* (puikiai, Vilniaus), root stress in *-iškai* words. |
